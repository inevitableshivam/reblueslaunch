How to plan launch-day distribution for a video

A practical plan for the founder post, supporting network and follow-up content on X and LinkedIn.

Distribution · Nov 2026

A finished film is an asset. Distribution is the plan for getting that asset in front of the right people and giving them a reason to act. Start with the buyer and the launch goal before deciding when or where to post.

Define the one action Choose what success means for this release: visits to a product page, signups, demo requests, or simply a clear introduction to a new category. The video, post and landing page should point to the same next step.

Prepare the founder post Write the opening line in plain language. Explain what changed, show the film, and give one useful call to action. Make a version for X and another for LinkedIn rather than assuming the same phrasing fits both audiences. Check that the first frame works without sound.

Build a relevant sharing list Start with people who know the company or serve the intended buyers: customers, partners, team members, investors and creators with a genuine connection to the topic. Give them the film and context in advance. A short personal note is better than a mass request for identical reposts.

Keep the first day responsive Make sure someone can reply to questions, thank people who share the launch, and direct interested viewers to the right page. Watch which questions recur. They may reveal what the next demo or tutorial should explain.

Use the film after launch day The film can lead the website, support sales conversations and become shorter follow-up cuts. The launch post is one moment in a longer content system. Keep the source files, captions and product claims organised so the material can be updated as the product changes.

No posting schedule guarantees views. A useful launch plan improves clarity, coordination and the chance that the right audience sees the work.
