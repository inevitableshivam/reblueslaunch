How Genlook turned one brand system into 120+ launch assets

Identity, a 43-second explainer, 42 ad variations and 17 short-form videos for an AI try-on app.

Case study · Oct 2026

Genlook is a Shopify app for AI virtual try-on. A shopper uploads a photo and sees themselves wearing the product before buying. The product needed to make sense to two audiences at once: shoppers who wanted confidence and merchants who wanted more sales with fewer returns. The launch system grew to more than 120 assets across identity, web, advertising, social and video.

One identity had to survive every format The identity began with a green star mark and a wordmark set in Stack Sans Notch. FK Grotesk supported it, while three greens and a gradient created a recognisable palette. The system then had to move from a favicon to apparel, stationery, profile images, web and out-of-home. That range exposed weak choices quickly. A mark that works only in a polished presentation is not a working identity.

The product proof came before the polish The 43-second website film walked through the try-on in two clicks. It showed the store, the try-on interaction, the difference between a page with and without the feature, and the data captured along the way. Every frame was storyboarded in Figma before animation. That made the film less about effects and more about proof: what the shopper does, what changes on the page and what the merchant learns.

Twenty-one concepts became forty-two ads The ad system used the product itself as the creative source: the try-on widget, the AI model studio and the analytics. Headlines ranged from "Boost sales" to "Reduce returns." Each of the 21 square concepts was rebuilt in landscape. The copy was reflowed for the new placement rather than simply shrunk. That distinction is small in production and large in the feed; every format still needed to read at a glance.

Different buying moments needed different content Two carousels handled longer explanations: one moved from the returns problem to the fix, while another compared four Shopify apps for stores with traffic but weak conversion. LinkedIn and web banners used before-and-after structures. Founder talking-head videos answered objections such as "virtual try-on is just a gimmick." Creator videos demonstrated the Shopify listing and a live store. Six AI-generated explainers addressed hesitation and returns. Phone-first screen recordings put the hook in the first second and the product immediately after it.

Scale came from the system The project included a logo and guidelines, one website explainer, 42 ad variations, carousels, banners, thumbnails and 17 short-form videos. Those pieces did not need separate visual ideas. They needed one visual language strong enough to keep producing. That is the real value of a launch system: not that every asset looks identical, but that every new format still feels recognisably yours.
