How 10x turned three launch films into 791K views

A crowded AI category, a founder-led hook, and one story that evolved across three launches in four months.

Case study · Oct 2026

10x is an AI app builder that can build, test and take a native iOS app to the App Store. The product was strong, but the category was crowded. A new AI app builder appeared on X almost every week, usually with the same claims: build faster, skip code, ship with AI. The launch could not sound like a better version of the same thing.

The brief was category, not features 10x did automated testing, competitor research, App Store submission and launch content. Listed one after another, those features read like a product page. The stronger frame was bigger and easier to repeat: Shopify for apps. That line changed the story from a tool into an opportunity. It gave buyers a category they already understood and gave every launch a phrase that could travel beyond the film.

The founder became the first hook The first film opened with Evan Yadegari saying, "I'm 14, and I built a $10K MRR app." The founder was not supporting material. He was the story. That sentence earned attention before the product explanation began. It also made the next claim - the first open-source AI app builder - feel specific rather than generic. The film could then move into the product without spending its opening seconds on a logo or a feature list.

Three launches carried one evolving narrative The second film reframed the opportunity again: "Building apps in 2026 is like dropshipping in 2016." Founders appeared on camera while motion graphics carried one product feature per beat. The story was not just that 10x could build an app. It was that more people could build an app business. Four months after the first launch, 10x Web opened by acknowledging the story already in motion: "Three months ago, we launched 10x." The old limitation - needing a MacBook - became the setup. The payoff was the ability to build, test and publish from any device.

What the feed gave back Across the three launch posts, public view counts on X reached more than 791,000: - 10x in April: 472K views. - 10x.app in May: 199K views. - 10x Web in August: 120K views. The combined posts also recorded 2,464 likes, 1,413 bookmarks, 373 replies and 112 reposts. The bookmarks matter: people did not only watch the launch. They saved the opportunity for later.

The useful lesson Repeated launches do not need repeated explanations. They need a consistent category and a new reason to care each time. For 10x, the frame stayed stable while the proof evolved: open source, Shopify for apps, then build from anywhere. The film changed because the product changed. The story held because the category was clear.
