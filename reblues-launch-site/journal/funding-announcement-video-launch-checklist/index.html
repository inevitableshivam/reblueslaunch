A funding announcement video launch checklist

Plan the story, film and distribution around the announcement without turning the round into the whole message.

Launch planning · Nov 2026

A funding announcement film should explain what the round makes possible. The amount may earn a moment of attention, but customers and future teammates need to understand the mission, product and next step.

Start with the audience Decide who should care about the announcement: customers, candidates, partners, investors or a specific professional community. Write down what each group already knows and what they need to learn. One primary audience should lead the film; other audiences can receive tailored cutdowns and post copy.

Find the story behind the number Ask what the company can build or change because of the funding. What progress made this moment possible? What proof can you show today? A clear answer gives the film more substance than a funding graphic alone.

Choose a format that fits the proof Motion graphics can explain a complex product or future vision. A founder-led film can make the decision and mission personal. Cinematic footage can show the people or world the company serves. The format should come from the story and the assets available, not from a trend.

Prepare the whole launch surface Before the release, approve the film, opening frame, captions, landing page, founder post and follow-up cuts. Make the call to action explicit. Prepare different suggested messages for the people who may want to share the news; they should be free to write in their own voice.

Plan distribution without promising a result Identify customers, team members, investors, partners and relevant creators who already have a reason to care. Give them context early, agree on timing and remain available to answer questions when the announcement goes live. Reposts and views cannot be guaranteed.

The useful test is whether someone unfamiliar with the company can explain what changes next after watching. If they can, the film has done more than celebrate a number.
