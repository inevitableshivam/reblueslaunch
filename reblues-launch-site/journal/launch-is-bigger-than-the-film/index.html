The launch is bigger than the film

The Reblues system for planning the story, film, post and first hour as one launch instead of four handoffs.

Playbook · Oct 2026

The film is roughly a third of the launch. The frame it enters, the post it rides on and the people who share it in the first hour decide the rest. That is why a clear product can still launch with a clear film and go nowhere. The film may explain the product, but the launch has no hook, no single owner and no plan for reach.

Five stages, one sign-off each The Reblues process moves through Discover, Write, Make, Launch and Amplify. Each stage ends with one thing to approve before the next begins: 1. Discover ends with the positioning brief. 2. Write ends with the locked script and storyboard. 3. Make ends with the master film. 4. Launch ends with the cutdowns, post copy and run of show. 5. Amplify ends with the creator wave and report. The sequence is normally planned backward from the launch date across roughly three weeks. Tighter dates use the same stages rather than skipping the thinking.

Discover the frame before writing the script Four inputs come first: the product, the ideal customer, the objections and the launch moment. The goal is one line a buyer can repeat. The category matters because it is where buyers will file the product for the next year. A useful frame is specific, easy to say back and built around the strongest doubt. For 10x, "every AI app builder sounds the same" led to the frame "Shopify for apps."

Hook first, logo last Two seconds decide whether someone keeps scrolling. The film should open on a face, a product moving, the old way versus the new way, or the problem in one line. A title card spends the most valuable moment asking for patience. The typical sequence is logo, intro, feature list, CTA. The launch sequence is hook, problem, product, why now, then logo and CTA. Three hooks are written, one is chosen, and every beat is storyboarded before production begins.

Pick the day before the date A great film can still lose on a crowded morning. The slot is part of the plan. We look for a weekday morning when the buyer is active, avoid crowded launch days, and sign off the cuts, copy and timing at T-1. The post carries half the work. It needs three lines and one ask: what the product is, why it matters now and what the reader should do. The link belongs in the first reply rather than interrupting the main post.

Run the first hour to the minute The launch starts with the founder's account. The team and investors follow, then founder friends leave substantive comments, then the creator wave lands. The founder replies throughout the day. With Amplify, creators are matched to the ICP before launch and receive the film with their own angle. The plan watches which niches respond, shifts the later wave toward what works and ends with a report on reach, engagement, clicks and what to post next.

The film should keep working After launch day, the film can stay pinned, lead the website, move into sales follow-ups and investor updates, and be recut for LinkedIn or ads. To start cleanly, a launch needs five things from the company side: a date, product access, the brand kit, one decision-maker and the team present on launch day. Everything else can be planned backward from there.
