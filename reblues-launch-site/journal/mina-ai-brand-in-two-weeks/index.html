How Mina AI built a launch-ready brand in two weeks

From a blank page to identity, launch film, cutdowns, print and sales assets for an AI workforce company.

Case study · Oct 2026

Mina AI builds AI workers for manufacturing procurement. They read drawings, find suppliers, send RFQs and compare bids. The company needed more than a video: it needed a visual identity and a complete set of launch materials that could explain an unfamiliar product with confidence. The timeline was two weeks. The output included a logo, brand kit, 105-second launch film, 30-second cut, social cutdowns, print media and sales assets.

Start with a system, not a deliverable The logo was drawn on a construction grid, with a mark and wordmark designed to hold up on dark and light backgrounds. Space Grotesk carried headlines, Manrope carried body copy, and a blue palette sat against navy and black. That system was applied immediately across profile images, web, merch, stationery and business cards. The point was not to make a folder of brand rules. It was to make every launch surface feel like the same company from day one.

Open the film on the old way The first 30 seconds build tension around the work Mina AI replaces: follow-up emails, paperwork and drawings handled by hand. The film does not begin by announcing an AI workforce. It begins with the friction the buyer already knows. Then the product takes over. The second half moves through the company brain and the deployment of AI workers one at a time. The close makes the shift feel present rather than speculative: "The AI workforce is already here."

Storyboard before generating Every frame was planned in Figma before a single clip was made. The cinematic clips were AI-generated, but the direction was not delegated to the model. Shots were iterated until composition, motion and continuity supported the script. That separation matters. AI can produce worlds that are difficult to film, but it does not replace the work of deciding what each shot needs to communicate.

Ship the whole launch kit together The master film and 30-second cut were only part of the release. Social cutdowns, print, sales assets and the brand kit meant the same launch idea could move from the feed into a meeting, a profile, a business card or a follow-up. Nothing had to be invented after the film was approved. Launch day was distribution, not another production sprint.

What the founder said After delivery, Mina AI CEO Sara AlMahri replied: "That's absolutely PERFECT. Thank you so sooo much. I love it." The useful part of the project was not speed alone. It was coherence: one identity, one story and every asset required to ship it, built as one system.
